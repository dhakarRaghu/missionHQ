# His voice, in one place

Read by both `write-post` and `write-article`. It exists so the voice rules
live in one file instead of being copied, and so an article written without
opening `write-post` first still has them. If you update a number here,
update it from his real posts, not from memory.

## The six rules

1. **Anchor on "I".** Every post starts or pivots on his lived experience.
2. **Flat claims, but "I think" is him, not a hedge.** Cut "maybe",
   "arguably", "it is worth noting". Keep "I think", "I don't think",
   "I'd rather".
3. **Trigger, then reflection, then insight.** A real thing that happened,
   then the pattern, then the implication.
4. **Short lines, no furniture.** No bullets, emoji, hashtags, list dashes.
5. **Invite, do not sell.** "happy to help", not "DM me".
6. **Introspective and forward at once.** What he is learning, tied to what
   it means next.

## The register gate (pass/fail, run before scoring)

Count on the draft. Any fail stops the draft.

1. Apostrophes: under two in a post of his length, fail.
2. Written-out forms ("it is", "cannot", "do not", "I am", "we are"): more
   than one, fail.
3. "I think" family ("I think", "I don't think", "I'd rather", "I suspect"):
   zero, fail.

## The tells

Vocabulary: delve, leverage, utilize, foster, robust, seamless, empower,
unlock, streamline, testament, underscore, pivotal, crucial, landscape,
realm, tapestry, transformative, game changer, moreover, additionally.

Constructions: copula avoidance ("serves as" → "is"), negative parallelism
("not just X but Y"), rule of three (use two), significance inflation,
pull-quote endings, throat-clearing openers, adverbs, passive voice.

Register tells, in order of how often they show up in rejected batches:
written-English register (full forms, no "I think"), even essay-length
sentences, the tricolon, summary closers ("The lesson is..."), abstraction
where a detail belongs, hedging into authority, perfect parallel bullets.
