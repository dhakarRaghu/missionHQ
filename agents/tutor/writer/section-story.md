## This is a story article

This section is part of a real system's story, not a course lesson. These
rules replace the matching steps above.

- Open from the story. The first section opens from the event in the
  opening. A later section opens with its bridge: what the section above
  found and what was still unexplained, in one sentence. Do not repeat a
  sentence from a section above, and do not ask a predict question that a
  section above already asked.
- Never mention a course, a phase, a lesson or what he learned before. A
  term that needs explaining is explained in one clause here.
- Tell what the team saw, what they believed, and what showed them they were
  wrong, with the note's numbers and the conditions they were measured in.
  Then the mechanism, one level below where a blog post stops, as numbered
  steps. Then the fix and the numbers before and after.
- Show the source's own evidence: its code, its metric names, its config
  values, its traces. Do not write new code for him to run; nothing runs it.
- Who reported a number and how sure it is goes in the Sources list. Do not
  put a `<details class="evidence">` box in the section.
- No key-point box, no `<p class="why">` lead, and no hand-over sentence at
  the end. The next section's bridge opens from this one's result.
- The last teaching section ends with what carries over to a system of his
  own: the one decision or check, said once.
- Speak to the reader as "you". Never "he", "his" or "him" for the reader.
