Can you still answer three questions with numbers?

Three questions, each one arithmetic you saw this week. Answer each before opening the block. Use a scrap of paper. The point is not the answer. It is whether the constants and the units come back to you without the lesson in front of you.

Q1. The long prompt

Three streams are decoding. A 24,000-token prompt arrives. The cost model charges 0.03 ms per prefill token, 18.0 ms per decode step, and 0.12 ms per decoding stream.

a) Without chunking, one pass runs the whole prefill. How long do the three streams freeze?

b) With a 2,048-token budget, 3 tokens are reserved for the decodes, leaving 2,045 for prefill. What does one iteration now cost, and how many passes does the prefill take?

Answer sketch

a) 24,000 × 0.03 ms = 720 ms. All three streams emit nothing for that whole pass. That is the freeze the max inter-token gap catches.

b) Prefill slice: 2,045 × 0.03 = 61.4 ms. Decode side: 18.0 + 0.12 × 3 = 18.4 ms. One iteration costs 61.4 + 18.4 = 79.8 ms. Passes needed: 24,000 / 2,045 = 11.7, so 12 passes. Every stream still gets a token each iteration; the new request pays with a slower TTFT of about 12 × 79.8 ≈ 957 ms.

Q2. The rolling update

A Deployment runs replicas=2 with maxSurge 50% and maxUnavailable 0%. You push a new image.

a) How many pods run at the peak of the rollout?

b) When does the first v1 pod die?

Answer sketch

a) maxSurge 50% of 2 is 1 extra pod, so the peak is 3 pods: 2 × v1 plus 1 × v2 coming up.

b) The first v1 dies only after the new v2 pod passes its readiness probe. maxUnavailable 0% forbids dipping below 2 ready pods, so the controller must wait for the v2 pod to report Ready before it terminates anything. With initialDelaySeconds: 60 in this chart, that wait is at least a minute.

Q3. The doubled bill

Your multimodal meter sees one request: usage.input_tokens is 300, and a nested detail says image.input_tokens is 200. Your meter bills 300 + 200 = 500.

a) What is the real billed input?

b) Which direction did the error go, and by how much?

Answer sketch

a) 300 input tokens. The 200 image tokens live inside the 300; the detail decomposes the total, it never adds to it.

b) Upward, by 200 tokens. The meter over-reports the request by 67%, and it can report up to double the real cost on multimodal traffic. Bill the total, price the subsets.

Three numbers from memory: 720 ms, 3 pods, 300 billed.

Does the path change?

The path changes only when the evidence crosses the bar you set for it.

Something new shows up in your study and it feels important. The temptation is to rebuild the course around it on the spot. That is how a plan becomes whatever you read last. So the lesson path has a rule: a topic must appear three separate times in your material before it earns a place of its own. One appearance is a signal, not a pattern. Three appearances means the idea keeps surviving contact with different lessons, which is the evidence that it will pay back a dedicated course.

This week the frontier shortlist holds exactly one entry: rare-failure verification. It appeared once, in the lesson on declaring a rare bug dead. The keepers from that week summarise it as its own rule: verify a rare-failure fix by counting the dangerous condition, not the damage. That is one appearance, and the bar is three.
